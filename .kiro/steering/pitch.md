# The pitch

Use with `/pitch` whenever you write the README, the Devpost text, the demo script or page copy, so they all tell the same story.

- **One line:** Tell it your budget, your clock and your nerve. It finds the costume.
- **Problem:** It's 5pm on 31 October. You have $25, half an hour and no costume. Idea sites give you pictures, not a plan you can afford tonight.
- **Audience:** DIY and last-minute costume makers: students, parents dressing kids, anyone heading to a party who'd rather make than buy.
- **What it does:** 8 hand-written costume builds with materials and methods. A lab that mixes them into 1,728 numbered creations with priced shopping lists. A finder that searches all of them by budget, build time and fright level.
- **The memorable moment:** set $25 and buy new, and nothing fits. The studio goes quiet and offers "Switch to Thrift: 54 fit". One tap, and the rest of the lab answers in the same moment.
- **Why it's a Costume Contest entry:** the spookiness carries meaning. In Lights out mode the spook dots burn brighter the scarier the build, a summoned costume materialises out of a blur, and dead ends read as a hush.
- **Shareable:** every creation has an address (`#lab=137&mode=thrift`). "Mine's No. 137, what's yours?"
- **Kiro story (keep it exactly this honest):** the first cut was hand-built in the first four commits. Every change since went through Kiro: steering docs first, then one spec per feature, a save hook running the whole suite, and Playwright through MCP for screenshots. The test suite checks all 1,728 × 3 against a pre-change baseline, plus seeded property tests and a copy-consistency check.
- **Facts to keep straight:** no backend, no tracking, no accounts. One self-contained `index.html` of about 5 MB (images embedded). Prices are rough US-dollar DIY estimates. Copy uses British spelling.
- **Don't claim:** that the project started from a spec, live prices, saved wardrobes, or anything else not in the repo.
